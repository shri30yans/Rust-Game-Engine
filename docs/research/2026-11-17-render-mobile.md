# Decide at cook time, keep pixels on tile

> Dated evidence, not the source of truth. The current design is in the topic docs listed in [../README.md](../README.md) §1; old doc names and decision numbers cited below refer to docs merged on 2026-11-17 (see [../README.md](../README.md#7-former-decision-numbers) §7).

The evidence points one way. Unreal-like image quality at 60–120 fps on phones does not come from porting Nanite and Lumen. It comes from decisions made once, at cook or device bind, about **what each surface is** (opaque, masked or translucent), **what geometry and texture format it uses**, and **which pass it belongs to**, and from a frame graph that never lets a pixel leave tile memory without a reason. The strongest numbers in the research back this up. Godot cut external memory writes about **300×** just by deriving load and store ops from the graph ([Arm](https://developer.arm.com/community/arm-community-blogs/b/mobile-graphics-and-gaming-blog/posts/optimizing-3d-scenes-in-godot-on-arm-gpus)). Merged subpasses use about **55% fewer tiles** on Mali-G76 ([Khronos sample](https://docs.vulkan.org/samples/latest/samples/performance/subpasses/README.html)). Specialisation constants saved about **7 ms** on a Pixel 8 Pro ([Arm](https://developer.arm.com/community/arm-community-blogs/b/mobile-graphics-and-gaming-blog/posts/optimizing-3d-scenes-in-godot-on-arm-gpus-part-2)). Each of these is larger than anything the headline techniques measurably delivered on a phone. The headline techniques fare worse when tested there. Nanite on a 2024 flagship Mali reaches only **30–40 fps**, and only with masked and WPO materials removed ([Arm](https://developer.arm.com/community/arm-community-blogs/b/mobile-graphics-and-gaming-blog/posts/mori-to-nanite-billions-of-triangles-on-mobile)). Lumen is not supported on Unreal's mobile renderer at all ([Epic](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/lumen-technical-details-in-unreal-engine)). Desktop two-pass HZB occlusion costs **more than 0.5 ms** on Adreno for the render-pass split alone ([Vulkanised 2026](https://vulkan.org/user/pages/09.events/vulkanised-2026/1415-Lucas-Silva-DevshGraphicsProgramming.pptx.pdf)). The engine's existing direction is right: a render graph compiled once, subpasses and transient attachments, CPU culling feeding indirect draws, an Enlighten-like light cache, and cook-time validation. Three planned items should change. The GPU-driven path's two-pass HZB, opaque and alpha-tested geometry sharing one pass, and the default ASTC 4×4 texture format all cost more on tilers than the alternatives below. The one change that also answers the owner's specific problem is a cooked, typed `SurfaceClass` per material section. It is checked against the actual texture alpha, and it fixes which pass family the section draws in.

## How to read the evidence in this report

Almost no source measured anything on this engine's hardware, so every claim carries one of three labels. **Measured** means a named party timed it on named hardware and published the number. **Claimed** means a vendor or author states a result without a reproducible setup, or the number came through a search summary of a page that did not render. **Inferred** means this report's reasoning from architecture, from desktop or console numbers, or from arithmetic. No source here measured the engine's two test phones (RMX5085 and RMX3853). Every budget below stays a target until the bench in `vk-0-phone-baseline` produces numbers, as AGENTS.md tenet 8 requires. Where the research notes flagged a source as unverified (for example Nanite DAG details taken from memory of the Karis 2021 talk rather than the PDF), this report leaves the figure out.

## Nanite's phone win is culling and LOD, not the visibility buffer

### What Nanite actually is

Nanite cuts meshes into **128-triangle clusters** ([Tricky Bits](https://trickybitsblog.github.io/2024/04/20/nanite.html)) and builds them into a DAG. Groups of clusters are simplified with their shared borders locked, then split again. Errors are made monotonic up the DAG so that each cluster can decide on its own, in parallel, whether to draw. The result is a crack-free cut at about one pixel of error ([zeux](https://zeux.io/2025/09/30/billions-of-triangles-in-minutes/)). Each frame, persistent-thread culling tests clusters against last frame's single-channel FP16 HZB, draws the survivors, and rebuilds the HZB. It then re-tests the failures in a post pass ([Tricky Bits](https://trickybitsblog.github.io/2024/04/20/nanite.html)). Visible clusters are written into a **64-bit visibility buffer** (32-bit depth in the high bits, a 32-bit cluster and triangle ID below) with an atomic max. Small triangles go through a compute rasterizer, which Tricky Bits estimates is about 3–4× faster because a one-pixel triangle wastes three-quarters of a 2×2 hardware quad. Large triangles go through the hardware rasterizer. Shading has moved from one full-screen depth-tested pass per material to compute "shading bins" ([Arm, Mar 2026](https://developer.arm.com/community/arm-community-blogs/b/mobile-graphics-and-gaming-blog/posts/mali-and-unreal-engine-s-nanite-enabling-the-future-of-mobile-graphics)). The idea that makes Nanite work is the DAG. The rasterizer and visibility buffer exist to make the resulting pixel-sized triangles cheap.

### What happens on a phone

Stock Unreal still does not ship Nanite on its mobile renderer. Arm got it running by patching UE 5.5.2's desktop renderer onto Android, which needed 64-bit image atomics, compute-shader derivatives and Mali driver R49 or newer ([Arm](https://developer.arm.com/community/arm-community-blogs/b/mobile-graphics-and-gaming-blog/posts/mali-and-unreal-engine-s-nanite-enabling-the-future-of-mobile-graphics)). Arm's **measured** results on a Vivo X200 Pro (Immortalis-G925 MC12) show where the value lies:

| Test (Arm, Immortalis-G925) | Without Nanite | With Nanite |
|---|---|---|
| 634K-triangle proxy trees, 18M max rendered | 199.36 ms (5 fps) | 26.16 ms (38 fps) |
| 76K-triangle trees | 37.40 ms | 22.75 ms |
| Primitives into hardware raster, 5M-tri scene | 58.67M | 10.17M (HW only), 0.03M (HW + compute raster) |

Source: [Arm, Mori to Nanite](https://developer.arm.com/community/arm-community-blogs/b/mobile-graphics-and-gaming-blog/posts/mori-to-nanite-billions-of-triangles-on-mobile).

The software-raster threshold sweep moved the Mori demo only from **25 to 34 fps** at its best setting (32–64 px), and a wrong setting (128 px) made it worse. Materials mattered more. The unoptimised scene ran at about **15 fps**, about 25 fps with world-position offset disabled, and **35–40 fps** with masked materials also removed. Arm notes that masked-out pixels cost about as much as shaded ones. Dropping resolution from 80% to 50% gained 21–32% ([Arm](https://developer.arm.com/community/arm-community-blogs/b/mobile-graphics-and-gaming-blog/posts/mori-to-nanite-billions-of-triangles-on-mobile)). Arm also warns that compute shading bypasses AFBC framebuffer compression and reduces vertex/fragment overlap on Mali. It calls compute shading "not recommended for most situations" ([Arm](https://developer.arm.com/community/arm-community-blogs/b/mobile-graphics-and-gaming-blog/posts/mali-and-unreal-engine-s-nanite-enabling-the-future-of-mobile-graphics)). The Vulkanised 2026 Quest work found a visibility buffer "impractical on Adreno, esp the 600 series", because SSBO reads there are uncached ([Devsh](https://vulkan.org/user/pages/09.events/vulkanised-2026/1415-Lucas-Silva-DevshGraphicsProgramming.pptx.pdf)). Mesh shaders reach only **5.83%** of Android devices ([gpuinfo](https://vulkan.gpuinfo.org/listextensions.php?platform=android)) and are absent on Immortalis-G925 even on driver 49.1 ([DroidDeck](https://github.com/Droid-Deck/DroidDeck/issues/447)). On Apple, 64-bit atomics arrive with Apple9 (A17 Pro/M3) and support only min/max ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/metal/feature-sets)). Min/max happens to be exactly what a visibility buffer needs.

**Inferred conclusion.** On tilers, Nanite's measurable win is that cluster culling and LOD keep frame time flat as content density grows. The visibility buffer and compute shading work against tile-memory economics. So the engine should take the DAG and the cluster culling and keep hardware rasterization with fragment shading inside the main render pass. GRAPHICS.md §1 already says the engine's maps are "buildings, not photo-scanned cliffs". That lowers the payoff of a full DAG further for IMAGINE, and it is why the plan below stages the DAG after the cheaper wins.

### Recommendation

Build the work in three stages, each a tier of one pipeline rather than a second path. **V1** cooks meshoptimizer clusters (64 vertices, 64–124 triangles, with sphere and cone bounds) and quantized vertex streams. Culling tests whole clusters on the CPU (later in compute), and draws go out as one `vkCmdDrawIndexedIndirect` per pipeline bin. **V2** adds the cluster-LOD DAG (`meshopt_partitionClusters`, then `simplify` with `LockBorder | Sparse | ErrorAbsolute`, then re-cluster, following `clusterlod.h`) with fixed-size streamed pages. zeux processed a 1.64B-triangle scene this way in about 2.5 minutes ([zeux](https://zeux.io/2025/09/30/billions-of-triangles-in-minutes/)), so the offline build is a solved problem. The runtime hierarchy traversal is not solved. Bevy's author shipped three releases of Rust virtual geometry and stopped before BVH culling was finished ([jms55](https://jms55.github.io/posts/2025-03-27-virtual-geometry-bevy-0-16)). Budget accordingly. **V3**, a 64-bit visibility buffer with a compute rasterizer for small clusters, is a research-grade optional tier. It is gated on `shaderImageInt64Atomics` and justified only if V2 measurements show sub-pixel triangles dominate. Content rules apply at every stage. Arm's own data says cluster geometry should avoid WPO and alpha masks, or switch them off with distance.

## Quantized clusters cut vertex bandwidth roughly threefold

Geometry compression now has two well-measured levels. On disk, meshoptimizer's vertex codec is **2–4× smaller** than quantized vertices. Its index codec reaches about **1–1.2 bytes per triangle**, and its meshlet codec 5–7 bits per triangle. All three decode at 3–10 GB/s on a CPU ([meshoptimizer](https://github.com/zeux/meshoptimizer)), and they are standardised as `KHR_meshopt_compression` in glTF. In GPU memory, AMD's Dense Geometry Format packs up to 64 vertices and 64 triangles into 128-byte blocks. It **measured 3.05–3.73 bytes per triangle**, for example 271 MB down to 41.3 MB for 12.7M triangles, while staying randomly accessible ([GPUOpen](https://gpuopen.com/learn/amd-dgf-an-open-geometry-compression-standard/)). Kuth et al. reached about 5.9 bits per triangle of connectivity using generalized strips, but decode in mesh shaders ([arXiv 2404.06359](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.06359)), which phones lack.

For a Vulkan 1.1 tiler, the right in-memory format is simpler. Store positions as 3×16-bit integers relative to cluster or mesh bounds (6 B), the normal plus tangent sign in octahedral 2×8 or 2×10 bits (2–4 B), and UV0 as 2×16-bit (4 B). That totals about **12–14 B per vertex**, against 32–48 B for float layouts. This is an **inferred** 2.5–3.5× cut in vertex fetch, and no source has measured it on Mali or Adreno. Positions belong in their own tightly packed stream, because Mali's IDVS shades position first and runs the rest only for visible vertices. In typical content, "half of the primitives are culled by the facing test" ([Arm, Mali-G710](https://developer.arm.com/community/arm-community-blogs/b/mobile-graphics-and-gaming-blog/posts/mali-g710-developer-overview)). Most of this is already in place. GRAPHICS.md §6.7 has a 12-byte position stream, an octahedral `snorm16x2` normal and Quantized16 cooked positions with per-mesh bounds, and shadow and prepass pipelines bind positions only. The remaining work: quantize procedural positions too, move cooked bounds from per mesh to per cluster when V1 lands, use the meshopt codecs on disk, and decode on the job pool at load. Do not build GPU strip decoding. Without mesh shaders it would need a compute decode pass, and that buys back memory the phone can afford.

## Keep the light cache, and plan for destruction from day one

### How Lumen works and why it does not transfer

Lumen traces rays first in screen space, then against mesh and global signed distance fields (or a hardware BVH). It lights the hits from a **surface cache** of per-mesh "cards" that is updated incrementally and gains a bounce on every update. It gathers the result through screen probes and a world radiance cache ([SIGGRAPH 2022](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2022/SIGGRAPH2022-Advances-Lumen-Wright%20et%20al.pdf); [Epic](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/lumen-technical-details-in-unreal-engine)). Epic budgets GI plus reflections at **4 ms for 60 fps and 8 ms for 30 fps at 1080p on current consoles**. Medium quality targets Switch 2 and mid-range PCs ([Epic](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/lumen-performance-guide-for-unreal-engine)). Fortnite "downsamples heavily" to fit 4 ms ([Epic](https://www.unrealengine.com/tech-blog/lumen-brings-real-time-global-illumination-to-fortnite-battle-royale-chapter-4)). Epic's docs say plainly that "the Mobile Renderer doesn't support Lumen". Android Lumen is experimental, needs the desktop renderer, carries a "significant performance cost", and has no published milliseconds ([Epic](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/using-lumen-global-illumination-on-mobile-in-unreal-engine)). Qualcomm's "Lumen GI at 60 fps under 5 W" is a **claim** with no breakdown. Unreal-quality lighting on shipped mobile titles today means lightmaps, volumetric lightmaps, reflection captures and a sky light.

### What the industry ships at mobile cost

Every shipping mobile-scale "dynamic" GI system the research found falls into one of two patterns. The first is precomputed visibility with runtime relighting: Enlighten, and Frostbite's precomputed GI shipped in FIFA and Battlefront ([EA](https://ea.com/frostbite/news/precomputed-global-illumination-in-frostbite)). The second is a baked probe volume with cheap sky changes, such as Unity's APV sky occlusion ([Unity](https://docs.unity3d.com/Manual/urp/probevolumes-skyocclusion.html)). The fully dynamic designs all trace rays into a surface cache, and none has a phone number. EA's GIBS surfels started at **6–7 ms** on PS5-class GPUs before optimisation ([EA, SIGGRAPH 2024](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2024/content/EA-GIBS2/Apers_Advances-s2024_Shipping-Dynamic-GI.pdf)). Unity's useful signal is a negative one. It **abandoned Dynamic APV**, a probe-to-probe propagation design, because it "failed to meet our standards for image quality and robustness". Its replacement is a ray-traced surface cache with a software BVH fallback, aimed at "high-end mobile devices up to PS4 Pro", and it has no published cost ([Unity](https://discussions.unity.com/t/feedback-request-changes-to-unitys-dynamic-gi-roadmap/1660897)).

### Recommendation

The VISION.md light cache stays the baseline. It has surfels with top-K precomputed links, an SH L1 probe grid, a progressive CPU solve on a job thread, and box-projected reflection probes relit from surfels. It is the only proven mobile-cost pattern, and it moves GI work onto an otherwise idle CPU core, which suits phones that throttle on GPU heat. It handles time of day, moving lights and emissives for free. Its weakness is the engine's headline feature, a **destructible city**. Precomputed visibility goes stale when walls fall. Three measures address this. First, invalidate locally: a broken piece's bounds are known when it breaks, so zero the links and probes inside those bounds and mark removed surfels as non-emitting. This is the "destruction masks" item already in VISION.md §2, and it should move earlier, because without it the light cache visibly lies after the first collapse. Second, treat debris and dynamic objects as receivers only, as Enlighten and APV do. Third, keep a traced surface cache only as a later high tier for hardware-RT phones. Note also that GRAPHICS.md §1 still describes GI as "tracing rays through a coarse voxel copy of the map". That contradicts VISION.md and ARCHITECTURE.md and should be corrected. The hidden risk the research flags is **link data size**: surfel count times K, per Director layout. Size it in `light-cache-1` before content scales.

For reflections, relit box probes are the bandwidth-cheapest dynamic option on a tiler. SSR needs a resolved depth and colour copy, which ends tile residency, so if used at all it should run at half resolution and only below Lumen's 0.4 roughness cut-off. Swapping Lumen reflections for SSR "saves 1 ms on Xbox Series S" ([Epic](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/lumen-performance-guide-for-unreal-engine)). The research found **no measured phone milliseconds for SSR, GTAO or contact shadows on Mali or Adreno**. GRAPHICS.md's High-tier budget table (GTAO 0.6 ms, SSR 0.8 ms) is a desktop target, not phone evidence.

## Culling on tilers: CPU first, never split the main pass

Desktop two-pass HZB occlusion is a poor fit for tilers, and the reason is specific. The second pass needs the current frame's depth in the middle of the main render pass. That forces a store, a compute dispatch and a reload. Devsh **measured** that the D32F round trip to "resume a renderpass costs > 0.5 ms" on Quest-class Adreno. Splitting the pass also discards Adreno's hardware LRZ from the first half ([Vulkanised 2026](https://vulkan.org/user/pages/09.events/vulkanised-2026/1415-Lucas-Silva-DevshGraphicsProgramming.pptx.pdf)). The same talk measured GPU-driven submission **without culling at an 18% penalty on Quest 2 (Adreno 650)** and 3% on Quest 3. With their tiler-specific occlusion added, the net gain on a hard outdoor scene was **only +5%**. Per-triangle compute culling was "counterproductive" because it duplicates the hardware's binning work. On the CPU side, Unity's NEON port of Intel's masked software occlusion still cost **about 4 ms** on a Galaxy S22 after a 3.75× speed-up, and was then abandoned ([over17](https://over17.github.io/2025/10/01/burst-occlusion-culling.html)). Unity's GPU Resident Drawer "slightly increases GPU workload", more so on lower-end mobile ([Unity](https://docs.unity3d.com/Manual/urp/gpu-resident-drawer-performance.html)). Feature coverage settles the submission design. Single indirect draws with `firstInstance` reach **98.29%** of Android devices, while `multiDrawIndirect` reaches 70.44% and `drawIndirectCount` 67.63% ([gpuinfo](https://vulkan.gpuinfo.org/listfeaturescore10.php?platform=android)). Native multi-draw on Mali starts at G710 ([Arm](https://developer.arm.com/community/arm-community-blogs/b/mobile-graphics-and-gaming-blog/posts/mali-g710-developer-overview)), and Bevy disabled GPU preprocessing on Adreno 6xx entirely ([Bevy #13323](https://www.Github.com/bevyengine/bevy/pull/13323)).

**Recommendation.** CPU culling over the SoA scene stays canonical. Today's renderer already writes visible slot IDs into per-mesh index ranges (GRAPHICS.md §6.7). Make it excellent: NEON plane tests over four bounding spheres at a time, split across the medium cores; a projected-size cull with a minimum size for gameplay-critical objects; distance LOD in the same sweep; and grid-cell rejection first. Shadows are where the bandwidth is. Fit each cascade's light-space bounds to the visible receivers and cull casters against that box swept toward the light. Cache static far cascades (GRAPHICS.md §4.2 already plans this), and use empty fragment shaders and D16 for depth-only passes, per [Qualcomm](https://docs.qualcomm.com/doc/80-78185-2/topic/mobile_best_practices.html). Submit one `vkCmdDrawIndexedIndirect` per pipeline bin. The CPU writes the arguments today, and a compute cull can later write the same buffer, so there is still one path. If city scenes later justify occlusion, add it as a stage that **never splits the main pass**. One option is a reversed-Z MIN pyramid built from the finished depth after the frame, tested next frame with the previous view-projection, motion-inflated bounds and Devsh's 2×2-footprint rule, falling back to "visible" on cuts and new objects. The other is Devsh's 1/8-resolution occluder plus occludee-box pass before the main pass. This contradicts GRAPHICS.md §6.1, where the GPU-driven path uses "two-pass depth pyramid (HZB)". On tile GPUs that design pays exactly the split the evidence warns against, and §6.1 itself already schedules a GX9 measurement of "two passes against one pass with last frame's pyramid". The research predicts the one-pass variant wins. Do not build per-triangle culling, hardware occlusion queries, conditional rendering (30.32% coverage) or mesh shaders.

## Upscaling: extend the TAA you have; let vendors plug in

On Vulkan Android the shippable temporal upscalers are Arm ASR and Snapdragon GSR2. ASR is MIT-licensed and derived from FSR 2.2.2, runs on any Vulkan GPU and shipped in Fortnite Mobile. Arm **claims** up to 53% more fps at 2× than native on Immortalis-G720, and about 25% less power ([Arm](https://developer.arm.com/community/arm-community-blogs/b/mobile-graphics-and-gaming-blog/posts/introducing-arm-accuracy-super-resolution)), but publishes no readable millisecond cost. GSR2 is BSD-3. Its two-pass fragment variant **costs 0.9–1.1 ms** at 1260×2800 output, against 1.8–2.4 ms for its compute variants, on an unnamed Adreno device ([Qualcomm](https://www.qualcomm.com/developer/blog/2024/10/introducing-snapdragon-game-super-resolution-2), via search summary). The spatial-only SGSR1 is about 0.2–0.5 ms ([mirror](https://rootjhon.github.io/posts/Snapdragon-Game-Super-Resolution/)). The neural options are real but tied to 2026 silicon. Arm NSS does 540p→1080p in **4 ms** in a demo setup, needs `VK_ARM_tensors` and `VK_ARM_data_graph` plus sync2 and maintenance5, and targets the Mali G2-Ultra NX, for which no retail phone is confirmed ([Arm SDK](https://github.com/arm/neural-graphics-sdk-for-game-engines/blob/main/docs/user_guide.md); [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/systems/2026/09/08/arm-pushes-agentic-ai-and-desktop-quality-graphics-in-next-gen-phone-platform/5294867)). Adreno Neural Fusion (disclosed September 2026) has Matrix Cores and a **claimed** 40% power saving over GSR, and its SDK's API, extensions and costs are unverified ([Tech Times](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/326531/20260903/qualcomm-adreno-neural-fusion-puts-ai-inside-mobile-gpu-unity-unreal-already-onboard.htm)). MetalFX Denoising is a black box that wants albedo, depth, dejittered motion vectors, a reactive mask and a transparency overlay ([WWDC26](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2026/359/)). FSR 4 has no phone path, and TSR is unsupported on Unreal's mobile renderers ([Epic](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/temporal-super-resolution-in-unreal-engine?lang=en-US)).

**Recommendation.** At 120 fps the whole frame is 8.3 ms, so only a roughly 1 ms upscaler fits (inferred). The engine's TAA, with jitter, motion vectors and Catmull-Rom history, is already a TAAU without the resample. Extend it into a **fragment-shader TAAU resolve that writes a render target**, not a compute pass writing a storage image. GSR2's fragment-versus-compute numbers and Arm's AFBC warning both point the same way. Use GSR2 and ASR as references for history rejection and thin-feature handling; do not vendor them as a second path. The engine-side contract matters more than the algorithm. Whatever upscaler runs, every frame produces:

| Input | Who writes it |
|---|---|
| render-resolution HDR colour and depth | the opaque passes |
| dejittered, max-dilated motion vectors | the prepass, skinned meshes, particles, and ribbon trails using previous control points |
| exposure | the existing auto-exposure pass |
| reactive mask (R8, MAX blend) | the translucent pass, in the same render pass |
| transparency/composition mask or overlay | the translucent pass |
| jitter, render size and max render size | the frame plan |

This set is the union of what ASR/FSR2, NSS and MetalFX consume ([AMD FSR manual](https://gpuopen.com/manuals/fidelityfx_sdk/techniques/super-resolution-upscaler/); [WWDC26](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2026/359/)). With it in place, MetalFX on iOS, NSS and Neural Fusion become optional backends behind one upscale node, each chosen once at device bind. The HUD and thin additive tracers draw after the upscale at display resolution. Allocate history at the maximum render size and vary the per-frame size inside it, recreating the context only when the surface changes ([Arm SDK](https://github.com/arm/neural-graphics-sdk-for-game-engines/blob/main/docs/user_guide.md)). Do not adopt frame generation for an aiming game. It holds back a real frame, and the [Lossless Scaling port](https://www.androidauthority.com/android-frame-generation-tool-pc-port-3665332/) measured 50–80 ms of extra lag. Treat VRS (Mali G715+, Adreno) as a later measured option. Its one published phone figure is "up to 30%" in Gears Tactics ([Samsung](https://developer.samsung.com/galaxy-gamedev/blog/en/2022/11/22/using-variable-rate-shading-to-improve-performance-on-mobile-games)).

## Textures: block size and framebuffer hygiene beat neural compression

Neural texture compression is real but not yet a phone technology. NVIDIA's NTC reaches about **5 bits per texel against 24** for a BCn material bundle. Its useful sample-time mode needs cooperative vectors, and it **measured 0.5–0.7 ms** of extra frame time even on an RTX 5070 ([RTXNTC](https://github.com/NVIDIA-RTX/RTXNTC); [Tom's Hardware](https://tomshardware.com/pc-components/gpus/benchmarking-nvidias-rtx-neural-texture-compression-tech-that-can-reduce-vram-usage-by-over-80-percent)). Ubisoft shipped neural materials in Assassin's Creed Mirage: BC1 latents and a one-hidden-layer MLP gave about **30% less texture memory**, but runtime cost confined it to a subset of assets ([Ubisoft La Forge](https://www.ubisoft.com/en-us/studio/laforge/news/415slmB3ZGzv8d2LhO9QT/shipping-neural-texture-compression-in-assassins-creed-mirage)). AMD's NTBC decodes to BC at load, so it saves disk only ([arXiv 2407.09543](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.09543)). No source gives a phone millisecond figure for any neural texture decode. On today's Mali and Adreno, decoding at sample time is very likely too costly (inferred). Decoding at load saves only what Basis supercompression already saves.

The large, certain win is block size. Every ASTC block is 128 bits, so bit rate falls from **8 bpp at 4×4 to 3.56 at 6×6, 2 at 8×8 and 0.89 at 12×12** ([Arm](https://arm-software.github.io/opengl-es-sdk-for-android/astc_textures.html)). GRAPHICS.md §6.5 selects ASTC 4×4 on phones, and UASTC LDR is a 4×4, 8 bpp format. Arm reports that astcenc improvements "enable you to use a 6x6 block size (3.56bpp) for normal maps" in two-channel `-normal` mode with `-thorough` ([Arm](https://developer.arm.com/community/arm-community-blogs/b/mobile-graphics-and-gaming-blog/posts/yet-more-astc-compression)). Basis Universal v2.x adds ASTC LDR at all 14 block sizes and **XUASTC** at about 0.3–5.7 bpp on disk, still transcodable to BC7 for desktop ([Basis README](https://skia.googlesource.com/external/github.com/BinomialLLC/basis_universal/+show/HEAD/README.md)). Choosing the block size per texture role (6×6 albedo, 6×6 two-channel normals, 8×8 or larger for packed ORM and masks) is an **inferred 2.2–4× cut** in phone texture memory and bandwidth, with no new runtime technique. Desktop BC7 quality after transcoding from XUASTC 6×6 needs a test. Framebuffer compression is the other near-free win. Removing the swapchain's STORAGE bit to enable AFBC **cut bandwidth from 788 to 529 MiB/s (about 33%)** in Arm's sample. STORAGE, TRANSFER_DST, MUTABLE_FORMAT, ALIAS and multisampled images all disable it ([Khronos sample](https://docs.vulkan.org/samples/latest/samples/performance/afbc/README.html)). On Adreno, compute writes "almost certainly" disable UBWC ([Qualcomm](https://docs.qualcomm.com/doc/80-78185-2/topic/mobile_best_practices.html)). Opt-in fixed-rate compression (AFRC via `VK_EXT_image_compression_control`) measured **65% less memory and 38% less write bandwidth** for a 2 BPC image ([Khronos sample](https://docs.vulkan.org/samples/latest/samples/performance/image_compression_control/README.html)). It is lossy and shows dither when sampled ([Ludicon](https://ludicon.com/castano/blog/2026/03/hardware-image-compression)), so it suits bloom chains and low-frequency intermediates, not textures. Sampling policy adds smaller wins. Trilinear costs twice bilinear on Mali, and full-rate bilinear of RGB/RGBA needs 16-bit samplers ([Arm](https://documentation-service.arm.com/static/620e22c90ca305732a3a5dd0)). Android recommends bilinear with 2× anisotropy over trilinear with 1× ([Android](https://developer.android.com/games/optimize/textures?hl=en)). `VK_EXT_astc_decode_mode` set to unorm8 was part of the Godot/Forge package that cut GPU time 10–20% ([Android](https://developer.android.com/stories/games/godot-vulkan)).

Removing textures entirely works for stylised art. MouseBot shipped many models as "100% vertex color" ([Vector Unit](https://www.vectorunit.com/blog-posts/2017/5/23/designing-mousebot-part-ii-toons-and-tech)). For IMAGINE's realistic target, the practical form is "few unique texels": low-resolution unique masks driving two to four tiling layers from a texture array, plus trim sheets, which GRAPHICS.md §3 already lists. Mip streaming as specified in GRAPHICS.md §6.5 is the right streaming design. Sparse residency is not guaranteed on the baseline, and the research found no mobile virtual-texturing costs.

## The opacity problem has a cook-time answer

The owner's problem, that the engine does not know whether an object, material or pixel is opaque, masked or transparent, is the same failure Godot ships. Godot infers transparency from shader code: in 4.5, any shader that writes `ALPHA` moves to the transparent pipeline, so depth-prepass settings silently stopped working ([Godot forum](https://forum.godotengine.org/t/transparency-sorting-issues-with-clipping-meshes-compatability/126960)). Its docs admit per-object sorting workarounds "may not always be sufficient" ([Godot](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/3d/3d_rendering_limitations.html)). Unreal and Unity avoid this by making the class a property of the material. Unreal's Masked mode is binary, and its clip value "is not designed to be changed at runtime" ([Epic](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/material-blend-modes-in-unreal-engine)). glTF carries the same three classes (`alphaMode` OPAQUE/MASK/BLEND with `alphaCutoff`), but exporters often emit the wrong one. A typical case is an RGBA texture whose alpha is all 255 being exported as BLEND (inferred from exporter behaviour; no measured failure rate exists).

The cost model explains why getting the class right matters so much on phones. On Mali, `discard`, alpha-to-coverage and shader depth writes "can force late Z-testing", and with late Z the hidden-surface-removal stages (FPK and its successors) "can be disabled" ([Arm](https://developer.arm.com/community/arm-community-blogs/b/mobile-graphics-and-gaming-blog/posts/hidden-surface-graphics-removal-methods-in-mali-gpus), via search summary). On Adreno, a draw with discard "will go to full-resolution Z in all situations". It cannot feed LRZ during binning, and alpha test has the same throughput as alpha blend ([Qualcomm FAQ](https://developer.qualcomm.com/sites/default/files/docs/adreno-gpu/snapdragon-game-toolkit/gdg/gpu/faq.html); [Mesa](https://docs.mesa3d.org/drivers/freedreno/hw/lrz.html)). Igalia measured a **13–16%** gain on a discarding demo from a better LRZ path alone ([Igalia](https://blogs.igalia.com/dpiliaiev/adreno-lrz/)). Epic's Fortnite team found masked Nanite foliage costly enough that "more opaque triangles, no mask" was usually faster ([Epic](https://www.unrealengine.com/tech-blog/bringing-nanite-to-fortnite-battle-royale-in-chapter-4)). The cost of a wrong class is therefore not one shader instruction. It is lost overdraw elimination for that draw and everything behind it.

**Recommendation: classify once, at cook, and make the pass a function of the class.** Each cooked material section gets `enum SurfaceClass { Opaque, Masked { cutoff: UnitAlpha }, Translucent { blend: TranslucentBlend } }`, with `TranslucentBlend::{Premultiplied, Modulate}`. Premultiplied covers both "over" and additive in one blend state, as Unreal's AlphaComposite does. The runtime has no "is this transparent?" query. The cook decides the class from the pixels, using the glTF flag only as a hint. It computes the effective alpha (base factor × texture × vertex alpha) over the texels the mesh's UVs actually touch. All opaque means `Opaque`, whatever the file says, and the alpha channel is stripped, which also allows a smaller texture format. Bimodal means `Masked`. Continuous means `Translucent` only if declared BLEND, and otherwise `Masked` with a cook warning. Every promotion or demotion is logged. An override file may force a class, but forcing `Opaque` on a texture with holes is a cook error unless acknowledged. Then shrink the masked set with the rasterization analogue of opacity micromaps ([Arm OMM](https://learn.arm.com/learning-paths/mobile-graphics-and-gaming/omm-mali-intro/4-build-and-validate-an-omm-adoption-plan/)). For each masked triangle, rasterize its UV footprint over the alpha texture. Fully solid triangles move to an `Opaque` sub-section, fully empty ones are deleted, and only mixed triangles keep `discard`. Build coverage-preserving alpha mips so foliage does not thin with distance, using Castaño's bisection rescale ([Castaño](https://www.ludicon.com/castano/blog/articles/computing-alpha-mipmaps/)), which is possible precisely because the cutoff is a cooked constant. The pass order is fixed. Opaque shaders are compiled with no alpha input at all. Masked geometry gets a depth-only prepass with discard and then a colour pass with `EQUAL`, no depth write and no discard. Then come sky and translucent. A misplaced draw cannot be expressed. This meets tenet 2 directly: the illegal state "opaque draw that discards" has no type.

Two current plans conflict with this. D29's pass list is "opaque (alpha-tested included)", which mixes discard into the opaque batches and poisons HSR for them; it should become a separate masked family. GRAPHICS.md §6.4 cross-fades LODs "with a dither during TAA". A dither is a discard, so every fading instance currently forces late Z in the opaque pass (inferred). Bin fading instances into the masked family only for the duration of the fade, which is a state the LOD system already knows, or remove the need for it once V2's sub-pixel cluster LOD lands.

For translucency itself, keep D30's sorted, premultiplied forward blending as the baseline. Additive content needs no sort. Exact OIT (linked lists, depth peeling) does not fit phones. Weighted blended OIT needs two targets and one geometry pass ([McGuire](https://casual-effects.blogspot.com/2015/03/implemented-weighted-blended-order.html)). Its accumulation targets can be transient attachments composited in a later subpass, so it costs almost no DRAM traffic (inferred), which makes it a fit for smoke and explosions but not glass. The quality ceiling is a k=2–4 layer blend (MLAB) in tile memory via `VK_EXT_rasterization_order_attachment_access` or `VK_KHR_dynamic_rendering_local_read` ([Arm](https://developer.arm.com/community/arm-community-blogs/b/mobile-graphics-and-gaming-blog/posts/framebuffer-fetch-in-vulkan)), and through imageblocks and raster order groups on Metal ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/tech-talks/603)). Coverage is not guaranteed at Vulkan 1.1 (`dynamic_rendering_local_read` reaches 28.19% of Android devices per [gpuinfo](https://vulkan.gpuinfo.org/listextensions.php?platform=android)), so it is a capability tier chosen at device bind. Soft particles should read depth as an input attachment in the same render pass. D30's glass refraction "reads a copy of the opaque image", which forces a render-pass boundary on tilers; keep it only where a tier's budget allows. The translucent pass also writes the upscaler's reactive mask with MAX blend in the same render pass, so the translucent class owns that output by contract.

## What Godot and Unity leave on the table

Godot's Mobile renderer improved by fixing basic tile hygiene late, under contract work from Arm, W4, Google and The Forge. Load/store ops inferred from usage cut external writes about 300× (PR #98670). Specialisation constants and fp16 removed register spilling and about 7 ms. Lazily allocated main targets saved about 50 MB. The Google/Forge package (dynamic UBO offsets, linear descriptor pools, immutable samplers, push constants of 16 bytes or less, ASTC decode mode, pre-rotation) cut GPU time 10–20% on Pixel 7 and S23 ([Arm](https://developer.arm.com/community/arm-community-blogs/b/mobile-graphics-and-gaming-blog/posts/optimizing-3d-scenes-in-godot-on-arm-gpus); [Android](https://developer.android.com/stories/games/godot-vulkan)). By its maintainers' own account it still lacks several things: vertex/fragment overlap across passes, mobile-optimal directional and soft shadows ("a large performance drop"), on-tile glow and DOF, GPU-driven submission, and thermal integration. It also hard-codes 8 omni and 8 spot lights per mesh ([Godot](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/latest/engine_details/architecture/internal_rendering_architecture.html); [Arm](https://developer.arm.com/community/arm-community-blogs/b/mobile-graphics-and-gaming-blog/posts/optimizing-3d-scenes-in-godot-on-arm-gpus-part-2)). Unity URP can merge native subpasses through Render Graph. But a Depth Texture or Opaque Texture setting, some callbacks and Compatibility Mode all silently split them ([Meta](https://developers.meta.com/horizon/documentation/unity/vulkan-subpasses/); [Unity](https://docs.unity3d.com/kr/6000.0/Manual/urp/render-graph-optimize.html)). Its GPU Resident Drawer excludes GLES and adds GPU cost on low-end phones. Unreal's mobile deferred path keeps the G-buffer on tile but is held to **128 bits per pixel and at most 4 input attachments** on Mali ([Epic](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/using-the-mobile-deferred-shading-mode-in-unreal-engine)). Exceeding 128 bpp raised Mali tile counts from about 262k/s to 410k/s in the Khronos sample.

A custom engine's advantage is that it can make these properties structural rather than a matter of settings. The `vk-2-graph` compiler should treat three things as errors, not suggestions: an on-tile attachment read by anything other than an input attachment or depth inside its render pass, a subpass colour budget over 128 bpp, and render targets created with AFBC-killing usage flags. Its report should print load/store ops and transient status, so tests can assert that Low and Medium run opaque → particles → sky → composite → UI as one render pass. Specialisation constants are already planned for tiers, and fp16 should be the default in fragment shaders. Barriers should use narrow stage masks so Mali overlaps the next pass's vertex work with the previous pass's fragment work. Culling compute should sit before the first render pass, never between fragment passes ([Khronos sample](https://docs.vulkan.org/samples/latest/samples/performance/pipeline_barriers/README.html)). Sustained-performance plumbing is where all three engines are weakest. The renderer should rotate in the final pass using `currentTransform`, pace with `VK_EXT_present_timing` (Android 17+) and fall back to `VK_GOOGLE_display_timing` or Swappy ([Android](https://developer.android.com/games/develop/vulkan/frame-pacing-extensions)). It should also open an ADPF Performance Hint session for the game and render threads, and let thermal headroom drive dynamic resolution before the OS throttles ([Android](https://developer.android.com/games/optimize/adpf/performance-hint-api)). The research found no published game measurement of ADPF benefit, so this is an inferred win that the 30-minute sustained bench in `vk-0` can test.

The order follows evidence strength per unit of effort, and fits the existing brief sequence (vk-0 → vk-3, light-cache-1 → 3). Each step is one canonical path with optional capability tiers chosen once at device bind, never per frame.

## Conclusion

The research changes what "Unreal-like on phones" means for this engine. Nanite and Lumen are not targets to port. They are evidence about what to keep. Nanite's DAG and cluster culling keep frame time flat as content grows; Lumen's surface cache separates trace rate from shading rate. The parts that make them famous, the visibility buffer, compute shading and per-frame tracing, are the parts that cost most on a tiler. The gains that phones actually measured all came from the same kind of decision: one made once, at cook or device bind, and enforced by structure. Load and store ops derived from the graph, subpasses that cannot be split by accident, shader variants as specialisation constants, and texture formats by role are all of that kind. The owner's opacity problem belongs in the same family. Godot's long tail of sorting bugs is what runtime inference produces, and a cooked `SurfaceClass` that chooses the pass removes that whole class of bug while recovering the HSR and LRZ savings the hardware already offers.

The second insight concerns where this engine can lead rather than follow. Godot, Unity and Unreal all treat tile residency, masked-geometry isolation and thermal pacing as settings that a project or plugin can quietly defeat. An engine whose graph compiler rejects an off-tile read, whose cook rejects a mislabelled material, and whose frame pacing is driven by thermal headroom gets those properties for every game on the platform, permanently. The open risk is the game's own premise. Destruction undermines precomputed light visibility, and precomputed light visibility is the only mobile-cost GI design with a track record. So invalidating the light cache locally when buildings break is the single piece of new engineering most worth pulling forward.
